# Detection Algorithm Example

## Scenario: Row 0 max_step changes from 31 to 7

### Timeline View
```
Step   | Row 0 Pos | Event
-------|-----------|----------------------------------
1      | 1         |
2      | 2         |
...    | ...       |
10     | 10        | [SysEx sent to change max_step]
10     | 10        | [RecordRowConfig called]
11     | 11        |
12     | 12        |
13     | 13        |
14     | 14        |
15     | 15        |
16     | 16        |
17     | 17        |
18     | 18        |
19     | 1         | <- DETECTED! Reset from 18 to 1
20     | 2         |
...    | ...       |
26     | 0         | (wrapped at max_step 7)
27     | 1         |
28     | 2         |
29     | 3         |
30     | 4         | [Verification point]
```

### Detection Logic at Step 19
```
prev_pos = 18 (at step 18)
curr_pos = 1  (at step 19)
new_max_step = 7

if prev_pos > new_max_step && curr_pos <= new_max_step:
    # 18 > 7 && 1 <= 7 → TRUE
    # RESET DETECTED!
    change_effective_step = 19
```

### Verification Calculation at Step 30
```
Detected change at step: 19
Position at step 19: 1
Current verification step: 30
Steps since change: 30 - 19 = 11

Expected position:
(1 + 11) % (7 + 1) = 12 % 8 = 4

Actual position from log: 4

✅ MATCH!
```

## Why This Works

1. **No Prediction**: We don't try to guess when the SysEx will be processed
2. **Empirical**: We observe the actual effect in the log data
3. **Accurate**: Position calculations start from the real change point
4. **Robust**: Works even with variable processing delays

## Alternative Detection Pattern

If the change happens at a wrap boundary:

```
Step   | Row 0 Pos | Comment
-------|-----------|----------------------------------
24     | 6         |
25     | 7         | <- This is new max_step
26     | 0         | <- Wrapped at 7, not at 31!
```

Detection:
```
prev_pos = 7 (new max_step)
curr_pos = 0 (wrapped)

if prev_pos == new_max_step && curr_pos == 0:
    # NEW WRAPPING BEHAVIOR DETECTED!
    change_effective_step = 26
```
